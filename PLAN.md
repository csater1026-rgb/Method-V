# Method V: Product Plan

**One-line pitch:** Method V is where builders show off what they've made, get real users, earn money from their apps, and find people to work with.

It's not only a demo-video app. It's a launchpad, a community and a career network for web app designers, software engineers and vibe coders. The 60-second demos, called **Drops**, are how people come in the door.

Items marked *(suggested)* are additions to the original idea.

---

## 1. Who it's for

| User | What they want |
|---|---|
| **Builders** (founders, solo devs, vibe coders, designers) | Traction, feedback, users, income, credibility |
| **Explorers** (early adopters, other builders) | Discover new apps, try them and follow builders |
| **Hirers and collaborators** | Find talented people by looking at their actual work |
| **Sponsors** (other apps on Method V, tool companies, later brands) | Get in front of builders and their users |

---

## 2. The six pillars

### Pillar 1: Drops (60-second demos)

A **Drop** is a short demo video of an app, like a reel or a TikTok. Upload button: **"Post a Drop"**. Tagline: **"60 seconds. Then try it."**

- A vertical, swipeable feed of Drops, each **60 seconds or shorter**
- A **"Try it"** button on every Drop that goes straight to the live app
- Separate feeds: For You, New, Trending and Following
- Filter by category
- Likes, saves, shares and comments
- Upload from your phone or desktop, with auto-generated captions and thumbnails *(suggested)*

### Pillar 2: Browse (App Directory)

- The main area where each app has its own page: Drop, screenshots, description, tech stack, links and team
- **Categories:** AI tools, Productivity, Dev tools, Design, Games, Finance, Education, Social, Health and so on
- Filters for category, tech stack (Lovable, Bolt, Replit, Next.js…), pricing (free, freemium, paid) and stage (idea, beta, launched)
- Search, plus collections like "Staff picks" and "Built this week"
- Real stats such as "312 people tried this" and feedback ratings *(suggested)*

### Pillar 3: People (Profiles and Networking)

- Profiles show your apps, Drops, skills, tech stack and links
- **Role tags:** Founder · Employee · Looking for work · Hiring · Open to collab · Freelancer
- **Similar taste matching:** suggestions like "Builders who make things like you" to help people find work and collaborators
- **Connect with a reason:** when you add someone, you pick why (Collaborate · Hire · Get feedback · Invest · Just a fan) and can add a short note
- Messaging after both people accept
- A jobs and gigs board where "Hiring" and "Looking for work" people meet *(suggested)*

### Pillar 4: Q&A

- A **Q&A tab on each app page** where people can ask the builder things like "Does it work offline?" or "What did you build this with?"
- Builders can also ask the community for help, for example "How should I price this?" or "Feedback on my onboarding?"
- Upvotes, a "best answer" marker and reputation points for good answers
- Tags that connect questions to categories and tech stacks

### Pillar 5: Reach (Getting More Users), the difference maker

- **Try-to-earn credits:** try someone else's app and leave real feedback to earn credits, then spend credits to get your own app in front of testers. Every new builder adds activity, so the site doesn't feel empty early on. *(suggested)*
- **Structured feedback:** a short form (what worked, what confused you, would you use it?) instead of just likes
- **Launch days:** a scheduled spotlight for your app with a countdown, featured placement and notifications to followers
- **Boosts:** spend credits or pay to get extra reach in the feed
- **Build in public:** post short progress updates that show up to followers
- **Share kit:** a ready-made "Featured on Method V" badge and share links for X, TikTok and LinkedIn *(suggested)*
- **Swaps:** two apps with similar audiences promote each other for free. See Boost Exchange below.

### Pillar 6: Earn (Revenue for Builders)

It works the opposite way from YouTube and Instagram: **sponsors back the app itself, not a creator promoting a product.**

- **Stack sponsors (start here):** tool companies like Supabase, Vercel, Lovable, Replit and Stripe fund prizes such as "Best app built with X," credits and featured spots *(suggested)*
- **Sponsor marketplace:** apps list their audience size and category, sponsors apply to sponsor them, and Method V brokers the deal and takes a cut
- **Backers:** fans tip an app, pre-buy it or become supporters, a bit like a mini Kickstarter *(suggested)*
- **Bounties and grants:** sponsors post challenges, e.g. "Build an AI app for teachers, $1,000 prize" *(suggested)*
- Payouts through Stripe Connect

#### Boost Exchange (apps sponsoring apps)

Big brands won't sponsor small apps early on, but **small apps will sponsor each other**, because they all want the same thing: users. This is the first sponsorship model, before outside brands.

**Two versions:**
- **Paid sponsorship:** an app with some budget pays other apps on Method V to promote it. For example, a note-taking app pays a study-timer app $50 to show "Sponsored by NoteFlow." The sponsor gets targeted users, the app being paid earns money even if it's small, and Method V takes a cut.
- **Swaps (no money):** two apps with similar audiences promote each other. Most early builders have no budget, so swaps get the most use at the start.

**Ways to promote each other:**
- **Sponsored Drops:** "This Drop is sponsored by ___," with a card linking to the sponsor
- **"Friends of" slot:** a small section on an app page for 1–3 apps it recommends
- **Embeddable badge or widget:** "Sponsored by ___ on Method V" on the builder's own site, which brings in traffic from outside Method V
- **Co-launches:** two or three related apps launch together on the same launch day
- **Bundles:** apps team up into packs like "Starter pack for freelancers"

**Making it work well:**
- **Matching:** suggest partners by category and overlapping audience ("Apps your users would also like")
- **Pay for results:** sponsors can pay per real "Try it" click instead of a flat fee
- **Honest stats:** sponsors see real clicks and tries
- **Clear labels:** anything paid is marked **Sponsored** (also required in the US under FTC disclosure rules)
- **Reviews:** both sides rate each other after a deal, so good partners build a reputation

---

## 3. How Method V makes money

1. Paid **boosts** and featured slots
2. **Pro profiles**, with analytics, extra boosts and custom profile pages
3. A **cut of sponsorships** (around 10–15%), starting with app-to-app deals on the Boost Exchange
4. **Job and hiring posts**
5. Sponsor-funded challenges and category takeovers

---

## 4. Trust and safety

- A 60-second limit enforced on upload
- An automatic **link check** so an app can't go live if its link is dead
- Spam and fake-account detection, and protection against people gaming credits and view counts
- Report and block tools, plus moderation for Drops, Q&A and messages
- Verified badges for real founders and teams
- Sponsored content is always labeled, sponsored slots are capped so the feed doesn't fill with ads, and only apps that pass the link check can sponsor or be sponsored
- Sponsorship stats count only verified tries from real accounts
- Basic legal pages: terms, privacy and content guidelines

## 5. Build phases

**Phase 1: MVP ("Show it")** ✅ built
- Sign-up, profiles and role tags
- App submission with a 60-second Drop
- Drops feed and the category directory
- Likes, comments and follows
- The "Try it" button and link check

**Phase 2: "Connect"** ✅ built
- Connect with a reason, then messaging ✅
- Q&A on each app ✅
- Similar-taste suggestions ✅
- A notifications system ✅

**Phase 3: "Grow"** (the difference maker)
- Try-to-earn credits and structured feedback ✅ built
- Tester Passport: stamps, tester ranks with perks, category badges, weekly streaks and a monthly top-testers board ✅ built
- Launch days and boosts ✅ built
- Build-in-public posts ✅ built
- Share kit and badges ✅ built
- Swaps and co-launches (free Boost Exchange) ✅ built

**Phase 4: "Earn"**
- Paid app-to-app sponsorships (Boost Exchange), with pay-per-try pricing ✅ built
- Stack sponsors and challenges ✅ built (challenges are created by the Method V team for a sponsor)
- Backers and tips through Stripe Connect ✅ built
- Pro profiles ✅ built (a $6, 30-day pass: stats, a pinned app, half-price boosts, a Pro badge)
- A jobs board ✅ built
- Still to come: both sides rating each other after a deal, bundles, paid featured job posts

**Phase 5: "Scale"**
- Outside brand sponsors join the Boost Exchange ✅ built (brands are verified by the Method V team before they can sponsor)
- A mobile app (iOS and Android): the installable app is ✅ built (home-screen install on iPhone and Android, full screen, offline page). Native App Store and Google Play apps are still to come; the installable app is the step before them.
- Analytics dashboards for builders ✅ built (7 days free; 30 and 90 days, and CSV export, with Pro)
- An API and embeddable widgets ✅ built (read-only public API, embeddable app card, badge)
- Native iPhone and Android app: 🚧 in progress (`mobile/`, Expo): Home, Drops, posting, Browse, app pages, profiles, and sign-in with email + password, Google and Apple. Next: inbox and messages, Test & earn, push notifications.
- Still to come: push notifications, write access to the API with keys

## 6. Suggested tech stack

- **Frontend:** Next.js on Vercel
- **Backend, database and auth:** Supabase
- **Video:** Mux or Cloudflare Stream, which handle uploading, compression and streaming
- **Payments:** Stripe, with Connect for builder payouts
- **Search:** Postgres full-text search to start, Algolia or Typesense later
- **Moderation:** AI moderation for uploads, plus human review

---

## 7. How to measure success

- Apps posted each week
- Number of "Try it" clicks, which matters more than likes
- Feedback given per user
- Connections made and messages sent
- Builders who got a sponsor, backer or job through Method V
- Boost Exchange deals made, and tries delivered per deal
- How many users come back each week

## 8. Open questions

1. Launch as a website only, or website and mobile app at the same time?
2. Is it free to post, with money coming from boosts and Pro? Or is posting limited?
3. Should Q&A also have a site-wide section, or stay on each app's page?
4. Should anyone be able to post, or should early access be invite-only to keep quality high?
5. Who are the first 50 builders to personally invite? The early community decides what the site feels like.
